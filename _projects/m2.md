## The problem

Some of the most valuable thinking in the metadata community happens live, in keynotes,
invited talks, and panels at DCMI conferences. Once the session ends, little of it remains
beyond slides and a recording. There are few citable, readable records of these talks, and
speakers rarely have time to write them up.

## About M2

[Metadata Matters (M2)](http://m2.dublincore.org) publishes concise summaries of keynotes,
invited talks, and invited panels from the Dublin Core Metadata Initiative. Each summary is
produced collaboratively by AI tools, the presenters themselves, and human editors.

**Submission criteria:**

- Must be an original contribution not published elsewhere
- Must reference actual tools and resources
- All co-authors must be aware of the submission
- All submissions are published under Creative Commons Attribution (CC BY)

**Editorial board.** Executive Editors-in-Chief from the University of Toronto, Stanford
University Libraries, Princeton University Library, the National Library Board Singapore, the
Bodleian Libraries at Oxford, and Sungkyunkwan University serve in an advisory capacity.
Regional Editors-in-Chief from the University of Barcelona, Stanford University Libraries, Uppsala
University, DataCite, and Chungnam National University carry out editorial review. DCMI's web and
community managers provide managerial and technical support.

## My role

As one of five Co-Editors-in-Chief, I review summaries before publication and help shape the
editorial workflow. I also helped build the publication's first prototype.

## Editorial workflow

The workflow is built around presenter consent and human review at every stage:

1. **Consent.** Ask presenters whether they're willing to publish their talks with help from
   appropriate AI tools.
2. **Record.** Let consenting presenters know their talks will be recorded, welcome voice-over,
   and offer an AI-assisted summary if they don't have time to write one themselves.
3. **Draft with AI.** Transcribe the recording, then use AI tools such as Google NotebookLM to
   draft a summary from the transcript and slides, up to four pages.
4. **Author review.** Presenters review and revise the summary, and may choose not to publish.
5. **Editorial review.** At least two Co-Editors-in-Chief review each approved manuscript.
6. **Author revision.** Authors revise based on editorial feedback.
7. **Final review.** Two Co-Editors-in-Chief conduct the final review.
8. **Publication.** The approved summary is published online under CC BY.

## The prototype

To test the model, M2's prototype content drew on the DCMI 2025 keynote panel
**Collaborative Production of Authoritative Metadata**, where I joined Tom Cramer (Stanford),
Simeon Warner (Cornell), and Dione Mentis (DataCite). Summaries were generated from the
session description and slides with the Stanford AI Playground (Claude), then lightly edited:

- **A panel overview:** how the field is moving from siloed, institution-specific metadata to
  shared, collaborative environments that keep quality and authority. It also covers the central
  question of finding the balance between the PCC model of authority control and Wikidata's
  open editing.
- **Talk summaries:** COMET, DataCite's framework for collaborative enrichment of persistent
  identifier metadata; my talk on Wikidata as a collaborative, open (CC0), multilingual, RDF-based
  knowledge base; and Blue Core as a shared BIBFRAME editing environment.

The prototype showed how AI-drafted summaries, framed by clear prompts and checked by presenters
and editors, can turn a live session into a readable, citable record.

## What's next

- Publish summaries from DCMI keynotes, invited talks, and panels as they move through the workflow
- Refine prompts, length limits, and review practices as the editorial team gains experience
- Keep authorship and attribution clear and transparent for both AI tools and people
